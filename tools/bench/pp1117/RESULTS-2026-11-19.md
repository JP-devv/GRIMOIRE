## Why Niluthon is ahead on Flash-Next (two cards), measured 11-19 on the Tower

Niluthon (github.com/Niluthon/qwen38-flash-tp2, vLLM patches, their README): MTP 3, 30.7 ms a step, 2.85 tokens a step
= 93 tok/s; prompt ~4,000 tok/s.  Their step trace (docs/two-cards.md round 11, 4 rows a target pass): target 27.9 ms
(MoE op 9.6, HC 5.4, GDN 3.6, all-reduces 2.8, MoE glue 2.4, router/shared 2.3, full attention 2.0), drafts 3.9.

Ours, v1.10.9 image, 8-bit, MTP 3, 164 experts a layer, pp512 (GRIMOIRE_SPEC_TIME, fenced per phase): snapshot 0.37,
draft 3.98, verify 38.12, commit 0.48 = 42.95 ms a step.  unitrace (prof-1119 = v1.10.9 bin + unitrace):
- plain decode: each GPU busy 12.5 ms of a 17.4 ms token (pp512): 28% idle.  Busy: INT8 GEMVs 3.5 ms, HC 3.5 (76% of
  bandwidth), VRAM experts 2.3 (~45%), the rest small kernels.
- small all-reduce (GRIMOIRE_TP_SMALL_AR_TIME): 96 a token, 24-30 us each = copy out 4.3 + peer wait 8-14 + CPU sum
  0.7 + copy in 7.6, with the GPU idle meanwhile.
- MTP step: ~29 ms of GPU work in a 43 ms step.

Why, in short:
1. Hardware: their 7950X + PCIe 5.0 x8 per card (~29 GB/s) + AMD root complex (GPU peer-to-peer, oneCCL inside the
   graph) + AVX-512 BF16 for the CPU experts.  Ours: gpu1 on Gen4 x4 (~6.5 GB/s) -- a prompt's RAM experts cost
   ~1.7 s per 8K chunk on gpu1 alone; no peer-to-peer on the 285H; AVX2 only.
2. Software: their whole step is one XPU graph (the CPU-expert handshake inside it).  Ours goes back to the host
   96-144 times a token (never graph-captured under TP); the GPU waits each time.

tools/l0wait_probe.cpp (gpu0): the compute queue can wait on a host-signaled Level Zero event (a wait in the command
stream, no spinning kernel): 5.4 us a round trip, every value right; with 15 small kernels between exchanges the
host-wait way costs only ~2 us more a round -> the exchanges themselves, not the restart, are most of the idle.
